About

I am an interdisciplinary researcher working at the intersection of epidemiology, data science, machine learning, economics, and environmental health. My research applies quantitative and data-driven methods to population-health, environmental, clinical, and policy questions.

My current work focuses particularly on environmental and spatial epidemiology, drinking-water quality and infectious diseases, climate and health, health equity, cancer epidemiology, and the application of machine learning and explainable artificial intelligence to health research.

Research

Environmental & Infectious Disease Epidemiology

Investigation of drinking-water quality, rurality, agricultural exposures, rainfall, climate, and other environmental determinants of enteric infectious diseases.

Cancer Epidemiology

Experience working with cancer cohorts, mortality, comorbidity, ethnicity, surgical treatment, radiation treatment, and systemic treatment datasets, alongside research on cancer care and health equity.

Machine Learning & Explainable AI

Application of Random Forest, XGBoost, survival methods, explainable AI and SHAP-based approaches to environmental, population-health, and clinical research.

Climate, Environment & Economics

Research on drought, climate change, agricultural profitability, land values, climate adaptation, environmental policy, and natural-resource management.

Current Research

  • Prediction of microbial contamination and related health outcomes in New Zealand drinking-water systems using a three-stage machine-learning approach.
  • Environmental and spatial determinants of enteric infectious diseases in Aotearoa New Zealand.
  • Climate and extreme rainfall influences on infectious-disease risk.
  • AI-enabled approaches to population-health, health equity, prognostic modelling, and clinical decision analytics.
  • Cancer epidemiology, survival analysis, and explainable machine-learning approaches to individualised risk stratification.
